PeoplePulse user handbook

Using the Attrition Dashboard

A practical guide for HR leaders, people managers, analysts, and senior decision-makers to interpret the dashboard and turn evidence into supportive action.

1. What PeoplePulse is for

PeoplePulse brings historical workforce patterns and model-generated risk signals into one place. It helps stakeholders decide where to investigate first, what questions to ask, and which retention themes may need attention.

It helps you

  • See attrition patterns by department and role.
  • Prioritise groups for further investigation.
  • Plan supportive conversations and interventions.

It does not do

  • Predict the future with certainty.
  • Explain every individual situation.
  • Replace employee, manager, or HR judgement.

Core principle

Use the dashboard as a starting point for curiosity and support, never as an automatic judgement or disciplinary tool.

3. Getting started

1

Open the live dashboard

Use the organisation's approved HTTPS link. Do not share the link or screenshots containing employee-level information outside authorised channels.

2

Begin with Overview

Read the total employee count, historical attrition rate, high-risk profile count, and the department and job-role charts.

3

Choose a question

For example: “Which roles show the highest historical attrition?” or “Where should we investigate workload and progression?”

4

Move to the relevant page

Use Risk triage for filtered prioritisation or Hypotheses & actions for evidence and retention themes.

5

Validate before acting

Discuss the context with appropriate HR and management stakeholders. The dashboard is evidence for a conversation, not the conclusion.

4. Overview page

The Overview page provides an organisation-level summary and helps users identify workforce hotspots.

Employees

The number of employee records currently represented in the dataset.

Historical attrition

The proportion of records where AttritionBinary indicates that the employee left. This describes the historical dataset, not a forecast.

High-risk profiles

The number of records with a model probability at or above the configured high-risk threshold.

Hotspot charts

Compare historical attrition rates across job roles and departments to decide where deeper investigation may be useful.

A group with a high historical rate deserves investigation, but group size, data quality, role mix, and local context should be considered before making a recommendation.

5. Risk triage page

Risk triage lets authorised users narrow the dataset using three filters:

FilterWhat it changesExample question
DepartmentLimits records to one department.Which profiles should HR review within Sales?
Job roleLimits roles available within the chosen department.Is a pattern concentrated in a particular role?
Risk bandShows Low, Medium, or High model-risk groups.How many high-risk profiles are in this selection?

Matching employees

Number of records matching the current filters.

Average predicted risk

Average model probability for the current selection.

High-risk in selection

Count of records in the High band within the filtered selection.

The table includes role, department, income, promotion history, overtime, satisfaction, probability, and band. Use these fields as prompts for a human conversation, not as a complete explanation of an individual.

6. Hypotheses & actions page

This page connects observed data patterns to practical questions and possible retention actions.

Job role

Compare role-level attrition rates and investigate workload, onboarding, leadership, and progression differences.

Income and fairness

Use pay patterns to prompt benchmarking and transparent reward discussions. Do not infer personal performance from income.

Progression

Explore whether limited promotion opportunities may justify career check-ins, mentoring, or development pathways.

Sustainable work

Use overtime patterns to investigate staffing, workload, flexibility, and work-life balance.

The page presents hypotheses, not proven causes. Treat each result as a reason to investigate with qualitative evidence.

7. Reading the risk score

BandProbabilitySuggested interpretationAppropriate response
LowBelow 30%Lower estimated risk in this model.Continue normal listening, support, and monitoring.
Medium30% to below 60%Signals warrant context and attention.Consider a routine check-in and examine local patterns.
High60% or higherPriority for careful human review.Offer a supportive conversation; do not assume an outcome.

Do not: tell an employee they are “predicted to leave”, deny opportunities, change employment conditions, or take disciplinary action solely because of a model score.

8. A recommended stakeholder workflow

A

Observe

Start with aggregate patterns and understand what the dashboard is showing.

B

Question

Form a specific hypothesis about role, workload, progression, recognition, belonging, or another relevant theme.

C

Validate

Check data quality and seek qualitative context from HR, managers, surveys, and employee conversations.

D

Support

Agree an appropriate, proportionate action such as workload review, development support, mentoring, or a follow-up conversation.

E

Review

Monitor whether the intervention helps and whether the pattern changes, while protecting confidentiality.

9. Data protection and access

  • Only authorised users should access employee-level risk information.
  • Use the minimum information needed for the stakeholder's task.
  • Do not copy employee-level tables into public presentations, email chains, or unsecured documents.
  • Store credentials and future API tokens in platform secrets, never in source code.
  • Keep a clear retention period and delete exports that are no longer required.
  • Use aggregated views for senior leadership wherever individual detail is unnecessary.

10. Common questions

Does High mean the employee will leave?

No. It means the model estimates a higher probability based on historical patterns.

Does Low mean no action is needed?

No. All employees should receive fair management, listening, development, and wellbeing support.

Can I compare two individuals?

Use caution. The tool is better suited to identifying patterns and prompting support than ranking people.

What if the result looks wrong?

Check the source data, report the issue to the data owner, and do not act on an unvalidated result.
